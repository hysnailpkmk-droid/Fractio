Fractions on a number line and in other forms

Every fraction is a number with its own place on the number line. The distance from 0 to 1 is divided into equal parts.

Numbers bigger than 1 can be written as mixed numbers, such as 1½.

One amount can be written in different forms: as a common fraction, a decimal, or a percentage.

Material 2: Equivalent fractions

Fractions that look different but have the same value are called equivalent fractions.

½ = 2⁄4 = 4⁄8

The rule: if we multiply or divide the numerator and the denominator of a fraction by the same non-zero number, we get an equivalent fraction.

12 = 1 × 22 × 2 = 24     48 = 4 ÷ 48 ÷ 4 = 12

Material 3: Simplifying fractions with the GCF

A fraction is in simplest form when its numerator and denominator have no common factor except 1. To simplify, divide both by their GCF (greatest common factor).

Example: simplify 12/16.

  • Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12
  • Factors of 16: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16
  • The GCF of 12 and 16 is 4.
  • Divide both by 4: 12 ÷ 4 = 3 and 16 ÷ 4 = 4, so 12/16 = 3/4.

Try it: which number can we divide both 12 and 16 by?

Iconic → Symbolic

Material 4: Comparing and ordering fractions

To compare fractions with different denominators, make the denominators the same using the LCM (least common multiple) of the denominators. Then compare the numerators.

Example: which is greater, 2/5 or 1/3?

To order three or more fractions, use the LCM of all their denominators.

Iconic → Symbolic

Material 5: Adding and subtracting fractions

Same denominators

When the pieces are the same size, we just count them: add or subtract the numerators and keep the denominator.

Different denominators

Pieces of different sizes cannot be counted together. First make the denominators the same, using the LCM.

Example 1: 1/3 + 1/4

Example 2: 5/6 − 1/3

Mixed numbers

A mixed number can be changed into an improper fraction: a b/c = (a × c + b)/c. For example, 2 1/3 = (2 × 3 + 1)/3 = 7/3.

Example 3: 1 1/2 + 2 1/3

Example 4: 2 1/4 − 1 1/2

Remember: fractions can only be added or subtracted when their denominators are the same.

Material 6: Multiplying and dividing fractions

Multiplication: a part of a part

To find 1/2 of 2/3, take the area model. Split a rectangle into 3 columns and shade 2 of them (2/3). Then split it into 2 rows and take 1 row (1/2).

Multiplication rule: multiply the numerators, multiply the denominators, then simplify.

ab × cd = a × cb × d

Example: 2/3 × 3/5

Division: how many pieces fit?

Dividing asks: how many pieces of this size fit inside? Choose the size of the piece to see how many fit into 2 wholes.

Division rule: dividing by a fraction is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal (flip the fraction upside down).

ab ÷ cd = ab × dc

A whole number is a fraction over 1, for example 2 = 2/1.

Example: 2/3 ÷ 1/6

Summary

A fraction is a number that shows a part of a whole.

Equivalent fractions have the same value even though the numbers look different. Example: ½ = 2⁄4 = 4⁄8.

  • Simplify a fraction by dividing the numerator and denominator by their GCF.
  • Compare fractions by making the denominators the same with the LCM.
  • Add and subtract fractions only when the denominators are the same.
  • Multiply numerators and denominators; to divide, multiply by the reciprocal.
